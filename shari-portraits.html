Portrait Session

Shari — Outdoor Portraits

Project Type
Outdoor Portraits
When
June 2026
Location
Mandolin Gardens Park, Houston, TX

Headshots, full-length and a few unposed — all in one evening outdoors.

Shari knew the photographs she needed and was open about not knowing where in Houston to take them. That is a normal place to start, and it is part of the job: I sent her a shortlist with the look each location would give, and she chose Mandolin Gardens for the greenery and water.

Her first instinct was midday, which I talked her out of. Noon sun is the least forgiving light there is for portraits. We moved to sunset instead, and a set that needed real variety — close headshots, full-length frames, a few walking and unposed — came together in a single hour.

She brought extra outfits, which is the simplest thing anyone can do to make one session look like three.

Mandolin Gardens — the quiet one nobody knows about

Shari lives out towards the Energy Corridor and wanted somewhere quiet that would not cost her half the evening in traffic. Mandolin Gardens is the answer I give most often for that, and almost nobody has heard of it. It is eleven acres tucked into the middle of a residential neighbourhood off Mandolin Drive, more or less invisible from the road — you turn into a subdivision, park, and find a proper park waiting behind the houses.

It also has a better story than most parks do. The whole thing was built on two connected stormwater detention basins, which the landscape architects rebuilt into ponds, islands and planted banks, then laid about two miles of fully accessible trail over the top with plazas and overlooks along the way. So the water you are photographing beside is drainage infrastructure doing its day job, and it looks nothing like it.

For portraits the useful part is how many different backgrounds sit within a few minutes of each other: open water, bridges and overlooks, deep green planting, and flat paved plazas for anything full-length. Shari needed close headshots, full-length frames and a few unposed walking shots in one sitting, and we got all of it without moving the car. It stays genuinely uncrowded in the evening, which matters when somebody is self-conscious about being photographed in public.

Two practical notes. The trail is level and paved the whole way, so it suits anyone who would rather not be scrambling about. And there is nowhere to change — I bring a pop-up changing tent to sessions here, which is how Shari got through three outfits in an hour. You can see how I photograph portrait sessions, look at where else I shoot around Houston, or get in touch.
