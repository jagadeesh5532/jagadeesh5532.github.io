Behind the Lens

It began as a hobby

Sixteen years ago I picked up a camera for no better reason than that I wanted to. There was no plan behind it. I simply found that I could not walk past a good moment without wanting to keep it, and eventually that turned into the work I do now.

What I love has not really changed since. It is the second before the posing starts and the second after it stops. A grandmother watching rather than looking at the camera. A swimmer’s hand hitting the wall. A one-year-old deciding, at last, to put both hands in the cake. The extraordinary hiding inside the ordinary — that is the whole of it for me.

So I photograph weddings and the proposals that lead to them, families as they grow, mothers before they meet their babies, christenings and Diwali evenings, sweet sixteens and seventy-fifth birthdays, swim meets and soccer matches, headshots and conferences. Different rooms, the same instinct: stay out of the way, pay attention, and be ready when it happens.

Home is Cypress, Texas. The rest depends on where your story is — a back garden in Katy, sand at Galveston, the Century Tree at College Station with someone about to get down on one knee.
