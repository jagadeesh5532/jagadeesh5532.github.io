Portrait Session

Nicole Thomas & Family

Project Type
Beach Family Portraits
When
2026
Location
Pleasure Pier, Galveston, TX

A family on holiday, the whole lot of them, on the sand at Galveston.

Beach family photography is its own thing. The sand moves, the wind does what it likes, and a big group spread across an open shoreline will not hold a formation for long — nor should it have to.

So this was never going to be a session of neat rows. The Thomases were on vacation together, which is the rarest thing a large family gets, and the brief was really just to keep up with them while they enjoyed it.

Children ran. Grandparents watched. Somebody always had sand on them. The photographs that matter from a week like this are the ones where everyone looks like they are having the holiday, not posing during it.

Nineteen people, midday sun, and the shade under the pier

We booked this for sunset. It moved to noon, which on a Galveston beach in summer is the hardest light a photographer gets handed: the sun is directly overhead, so it drops shadow straight into everybody’s eye sockets, and then the pale sand and the water underneath act like two enormous reflectors and throw the whole scene two or three stops brighter than it has any business being. Nobody can keep their eyes open. Everybody squints.

You do not argue with that when nineteen people have driven four hours to be there. There is no second date, no rescheduling for better light — it happens now or it does not happen. So you stop wishing for sunset and go and find shade, and on this part of the seafront the shade is underneath the Galveston Island Historic Pleasure Pier at 25th and Seawall.

Under a pier the physics flip in your favour. The deck overhead kills the hard top light, the pilings give you something to frame with, and the open sides let all that bounced glare off the sand and water come in sideways as soft fill. It is one of the few places on an open shoreline where you can photograph a face at noon and have it look like you chose the hour. The surf still comes through, so you work between sets and accept wet feet.

The other half of the job was simply order of operations. With nineteen people, the youngest ones have a finite amount of cooperation in them and it runs out early, so the full group goes first, while everyone is still fresh and still assembled. Then it breaks down — each household, then the grandparents with the grandchildren, then couples, then the kids left to get on with it in the water. Shot in that sequence you get everything. Shot in the reverse you get a large family photograph with four children crying in it.

If you are planning a beach session with a big group, come an hour either side of sunrise or sunset if you possibly can — and if you cannot, tell me and I will plan around the shade instead. You can see how I photograph families, look at where else I shoot, or get in touch with your dates.
