Maternity Photography

Unhurried outdoor sessions in the last weeks before everything changes.

The last few weeks of just the two of you

A maternity session sits in a narrow window. Too early and it does not show; too late and standing on a beach for an hour stops being appealing. Somewhere around thirty-two to thirty-six weeks is usually right.

These sessions happen outdoors around Houston and down toward the coast — the shoreline at El Jardin Beach on Galveston Bay, the trees and water at Oyster Creek Park in Sugar Land. We keep it slow, with somewhere to sit and plenty of time, because Texas heat and late pregnancy are not a good combination.

Bring a second outfit if you want one, and bring whoever else belongs in the frame — partners, older children, sometimes the dog. In a year these will be the only photographs you have of the house before it changed.

What you get

  • Sessions timed for late afternoon and the hour before sunset
  • Breaks built in, and somewhere to sit between sets
  • Partners, older children and pets welcome in the frame
  • Outfit and location suggestions beforehand if you want them
  • An online gallery with high-resolution files and web-sized copies
  • Normal turnaround is five to seven days

Questions people ask

When should we book the session?

Around thirty-two to thirty-six weeks for most people. Book the date a few weeks ahead of that so there is room to move it if the weather turns.

How long does a maternity session take?

Between forty-five minutes and an hour and a half. We go at whatever pace is comfortable — there is no reason to rush, and standing for long stretches is not part of it.

What should I wear?

Something that shows the shape rather than hides it, in a colour that works against sand, water or greenery. Tell me where we are shooting and I will give you specific suggestions, and a second outfit is always worth bringing.

What happens if it rains?

We reschedule to a date that suits us both. Outdoor sessions live and die by the weather, and there is no sense fighting it.

Can my partner and older children be in the photos?

Yes, and I would encourage it. Some of the best frames from these sessions are an older sibling with a hand on the bump.
