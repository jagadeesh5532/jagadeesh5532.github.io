What to Wear

Color palettes, shoes and accessories for photographs in Houston — by setting, occasion and season.

Start with the background, not the outfit

Almost every what-to-wear guide starts with colors you like. That is the wrong end. What decides whether an outfit photographs well is what sits behind you — and in Houston that is usually something very green, very bright, or both.

Choosing what to wear is the part most people worry about, and it is easier than it looks. This guide walks you through colors, fabrics, shoes, hair and what to bring, so you feel comfortable and look like yourself on the day.

So choose the setting first, take the palette that separates cleanly from it, then let the occasion decide how formal to go. Below is what I tell clients when they ask, which is most of them.

Five rules that work everywhere

  1. Pick the setting first, then the color.
  2. Coordinate, do not match: three or four colors from one palette.
  3. Skip green in parks and heavy black in daylight.
  4. Wear comfortable shoes you can stand and walk in.
  5. Bring one backup outfit.

Not sure about an outfit? Text me a photo and I will tell you honestly what will work.

Park or garden

Mercer Arboretum, Terry Hershey, Mandolin Gardens, Oyster Creek — anywhere green.

Oatmeal#DCCDBB

What works

  • Creams, oatmeal and soft neutrals — they lift away from heavy foliage
  • Warm earth tones: terracotta, rust, caramel, clay
  • One deeper anchor color per group, usually navy or burgundy
  • Light, floaty fabrics — linen, cotton, chiffon

What to avoid

  • Green and olive — you disappear into the background
  • Neon or hot brights, which throw color onto faces
  • Busy prints and logos, which pull the eye off you
  • Heavy black in daylight — it flattens on camera

Houston note. Houston parks stay green nearly all year, so this holds in January as much as July. Spray for mosquitoes before you dress, not after.

Beach or bay

El Jardin in Seabrook, Galveston, Kings Harbor — anywhere on the water.

Ivory#F6EFE4
Sand#E0CDB2
Sea Glass#AFC4C4
Dusty Rose#D7A99E

What works

  • Ivory, white and sand — they glow in low sun
  • Soft blues and sea-glass greens, which sit naturally against water
  • Long skirts and loose shirts that catch the breeze
  • Flowing fabric — movement is half the picture at the coast

What to avoid

  • Strong black, which goes to silhouette against a bright sky
  • Stiff, structured tailoring — it fights the setting
  • Anything you would be upset to get sand or salt water on
  • Brand-new bright white that has never been washed; it can glare

Houston note. Gulf wind is constant. Bring hair ties, pin anything that lifts, and expect to re-tuck between frames — the wind is usually a gift, not a problem.

Downtown or urban

Buffalo Bayou, Eleanor Tinsley, CityPlace — brick, concrete and glass.

Charcoal#3A3A3C

Burgundy#6E2F39

What works

  • Deeper, richer color than you would wear in a park
  • Strong tailoring — concrete and glass take structure well
  • Jewel tones: emerald, burgundy, rust, mustard
  • Texture — leather, denim, wool, suede

What to avoid

  • Pale pastels, which wash out against gray concrete
  • Tiny repeating patterns, which shimmer on camera
  • Anything you cannot comfortably walk several blocks in

Houston note. Downtown means walking between backdrops and a lot of heat thrown back off pavement. Bring the shoes you want in the photographs and comfortable ones for the gaps.

Indoors, home or venue

A living room, a hall, a hotel, a restaurant.

Warm White#F4EDE3
Taupe#B9A892
Soft Sage#9BA893
Plum#5E3F55
Gold Ochre#B38A3E

What works

  • Colors that work with the room rather than against it — look at the walls first
  • Soft, warm neutrals, which sit well under indoor light
  • A little sheen — silk and satin pick up window light beautifully
  • Layers you can shed once the room warms up

What to avoid

  • Matching everyone in one color — coordinate, do not uniform
  • Heavy pattern in an already patterned room
  • Fluorescent brights, which shift color badly under mixed indoor lighting

Houston note. Houston homes run cool with the air conditioning but fill up fast with people. Dress for a room that will be warmer in an hour than when you arrive.

Church or ceremony

A baptism, a christening, a Nikkah, a temple, a courthouse.

Ivory#F3EADC
Soft Gray#B6B3AE
Deep Navy#2B3A52
Burgundy#6B2E38
Antique Gold#B2914C

What works

  • Whatever your family’s tradition calls for — that comes first
  • Modest cuts: covered shoulders and knees are expected at most venues
  • Classic, quiet color rather than anything attention-seeking
  • Clothes you can sit, kneel and stand in repeatedly without adjusting

What to avoid

  • Anything that competes with the people the day belongs to
  • Noisy jewelry — it carries in a quiet room
  • Shoes that echo on a hard floor

Houston note. Check the venue’s own dress guidance before buying anything. If the ceremony is one I have not photographed before, tell me how it runs and I will plan around it.

Wedding

Guests: nothing white, and nothing that reads as white on camera. Wedding party: agree the exact shade together rather than describing it — “navy” covers a lot of ground.

Women

  • A dress with some weight to the fabric so it hangs well through a long day. Check you can dance in it.
  • Shoes: Heels for the ceremony, flats hidden in a bag for the reception. Grass and heels do not mix.

Men

  • A suit that fits properly beats an expensive one that does not. A tie you can loosen later.
  • Shoes: Polished leather, broken in. Dark socks, long enough that nothing shows when you sit.

Accessories

  • One statement piece is plenty. Watches, cufflinks and simple earrings photograph well; anything that catches light repeatedly does not.

Engagement or proposal

Two outfits is the sweet spot: one relaxed, one dressed up. Three is the most I would attempt in a single session.

Women

  • One flowing dress and one everyday outfit you actually feel like yourself in. Movement photographs beautifully.
  • Shoes: If he is proposing, you need to be able to walk there without knowing why. Comfortable shoes.

Men

  • Match her formality, not her color. If she is in a long dress, you are not in shorts.
  • Shoes: Clean and simple, not brand new. Boots or loafers over sneakers.

Accessories

  • For a surprise proposal, keep hands free and nails done — the ring photographs are close and unforgiving.

Maternity

Clothes that show the shape rather than hide it. Fitted beats flowing at the bump, even if the rest flows.

Women

  • A fitted bodice with a long skirt is the classic for a reason. Bring a second option in case you change your mind on the day.
  • Shoes: Flat, every time. You will be standing on grass or sand, and discomfort shows on your face.

Men

  • Simple and quiet — you are the supporting frame. Plain shirt, sleeves rolled.
  • Shoes: Whatever lets you crouch, carry and walk on soft ground.

Accessories

  • Delicate jewelry only. Nothing that sits across the bump or needs constant adjusting.

Family or portraits

Coordinate, do not match. Pick three or four colors from the palette and let everyone take one — it reads as considered rather than staged.

Women

  • Start with the hardest person to dress, usually a teenager, then build everyone else around them.
  • Shoes: Flat or low. You will be sitting on the ground more than you expect.

Men

  • Layers add depth: a shirt with a jacket or cardigan reads better than one flat color.
  • Shoes: Clean shoes. They end up in more frames than anyone anticipates.

Accessories

  • Dress small children last and bring a spare top for them. Hair ties on wrists show in every photograph — keep them in a pocket.

Headshots

Everything happens from the chest up, so necklines and shoulders are the whole decision.

Women

  • Solid colors, a neckline you are comfortable in, and sleeves — bare shoulders read as a holiday photograph rather than a professional one.
  • Shoes: Irrelevant. Wear whatever is comfortable.

Men

  • A shirt that fits at the collar and the shoulder. A jacket if your industry expects one.
  • Shoes: Also irrelevant. Nobody will ever see them.

Accessories

  • Minimal. Small earrings, a simple watch, nothing that glints. Bring two or three tops so we can compare.

Birthday or celebration

The one occasion to let the guest of honour stand out properly and keep everyone else a notch quieter.

Women

  • Wear the thing you have been saving. If it sparkles, it will photograph well under party lighting.
  • Shoes: If there is dancing, bring a second pair. There is always dancing.

Men

  • Sharper than you think the occasion calls for. You will be glad of it in the pictures.
  • Shoes: Comfortable — parties run longer than planned.

Accessories

  • Sequins, metallics and sparkle work here in a way they do not elsewhere. Lean into it.

Cultural or religious

Traditional dress always photographs better than a Western compromise. Wear what the occasion actually calls for.

Women

  • Full traditional dress where it applies. Rich color is the point, not something to tone down.
  • Shoes: Easy on and off if shoes come off at the venue, and comfortable for long standing.

Men

  • Match the formality of the ceremony. If the elders are in traditional dress, so are you.
  • Shoes: The same — and check whether you are expected to remove them.

Accessories

  • Family jewelry deserves to be photographed properly. Tell me about anything with a story before we start and I will make time for it.

Corporate or event

Agree a dress code across the team beforehand. Mixed levels of formality are the most common thing that undoes a company photograph.

Women

  • Business or business-casual, matched to your industry. Solid colors over pattern.
  • Shoes: Smart and comfortable — conference days are long and mostly standing.

Men

  • Collared shirt at minimum. A jacket if leadership is wearing one.
  • Shoes: Polished. They show in full-length and group shots.

Accessories

  • Name badges straight and the right way round. Lanyards twist constantly — worth a glance before each group photograph.

Senior portraits

Bring two or three looks that feel like you: one polished, one everyday, and one that shows what you love, such as a band uniform, a jersey or a favorite hoodie.

Women

  • Solid colors that separate from the background, with simple necklines. Bring a jacket or cardigan to change the look quickly.
  • Shoes: Whatever you would actually wear. Clean sneakers, boots or flats all photograph well.

Men

  • A shirt that fits at the shoulders, plus a layer such as a jacket or overshirt. Avoid big logos.
  • Shoes: Clean and simple. Boots, loafers or sneakers.

Accessories

  • Keep it minimal. Class rings, a watch and simple jewelry are plenty.

Quinceañera or Sweet 16

The dress is the star. Choose a backdrop that lets it stand out, and bring the whole outfit: dress, shoes, jewelry and any crown or accessories.

Women

  • Wear the dress you chose for the day. Bring a second casual look if you want one. Make sure the hem is clean and has been steamed.
  • Shoes: Bring the heels for the photos and flats for walking between spots. A long dress hides them in most frames.

Men

  • If included, a fitted shirt or suit in a color that complements, not matches, her dress.
  • Shoes: Polished dress shoes, clean and broken in.

Accessories

  • Tiara, earrings and a small bouquet work well. Keep hair secure for wind, and bring pins and a mirror.

Sports or team

Wear the full uniform, clean and ironed, with the right socks and shoes. Bring a second, casual outfit for portraits if you want one.

Women

  • Team colors, hair neat and out of the face, with a ribbon or tie that matches the uniform.
  • Shoes: Cleats or game shoes, cleaned. Bring slides for the walk between spots.

Men

  • Full uniform with the jersey tucked as your league requires. Bring the ball, bat, helmet or other gear.
  • Shoes: Cleaned game shoes.

Accessories

  • Bring whatever you play with, and leave out anything the league does not allow.

Summer (May to September)

Houston summer is the real constraint. Natural fibres only — linen, cotton, light silk — and avoid polyester, which traps heat and shows sweat. Shoot at sunrise or in the last ninety minutes before sunset; midday is unflattering and genuinely uncomfortable. Bring water and a cloth for faces.

Spring or fall

The best months here. Almost anything works, layers behave, and the light lasts. Spring brings pollen and wildflowers; fall brings the warmest light of the year. Book these months early — everyone else wants them too.

Winter (December to February)

Mild rather than cold, with lovely low light all day. Layer rather than bundle — coats come on and off between frames. One genuinely cold snap a year is possible, so check the forecast the week before and keep a fallback date in mind.

Fabric, fit and details

The small things that decide whether an outfit still looks good an hour into the session.

Fabric

  • Choose fabrics that hold their shape: cotton blends, linen blends, crepe, knits with some weight.
  • Steam or iron everything the night before. Wrinkles show in every frame.
  • Avoid thin, clingy fabrics, and see-through or shiny fabrics under bright sun.

Fit

  • Try everything on a few days ahead and sit, bend and raise your arms in it.
  • Clothes that fit well photograph better than expensive clothes that do not.
  • Check hems and sleeves. Too long or too tight both pull focus.

Patterns and details

  • Small, busy patterns can shimmer on camera. Larger, softer patterns are safer.
  • Skip big logos and slogans so the focus stays on faces.
  • If one person wears a pattern, keep everyone else in solids.

Hair, makeup and grooming

You will be looking at these photos for years, so a little preparation helps.

Hair

  • Get a trim or color a week or two before, not the day before.
  • Bring a brush, pins and a few hair ties. Wind and humidity are part of Houston.
  • If you plan to change your style between outfits, bring what you need.

Makeup and skin

  • Natural, a little more defined than usual. Camera tends to soften everything.
  • Moisturize the day before, and carry blotting papers and lip balm.
  • Use a matte finish where you shine, especially on a hot day.

Grooming

  • Nails: clean and neat, and especially important for engagement or proposal photos.
  • Shave or trim the day before, not an hour before.
  • Bring a small mirror and a lint roller.

Kids, babies and little ones

Comfort comes first. A happy child photographs better than a perfectly dressed one.

Outfits

  • Soft, comfortable clothes they can move in. Avoid scratchy tags and stiff shoes.
  • Let older kids have a say in one outfit so they feel involved.
  • Coordinate with the family palette, not an exact match.

Timing

  • Schedule around nap and meal times, and avoid the hour before a nap.
  • Feed them before we start, and keep the session short with plenty of breaks.
  • Dress little ones last, right before the photos.

What to bring

  • A spare outfit, wipes and a few favorite snacks.
  • A comfort item or a small toy to bring out a smile.
  • Water, and a change of shoes in case of mud or grass.

Does skin tone change what you should wear?

Yes — but not in the way people usually mean. There is no color that any skin tone cannot wear, and I would be suspicious of any guide that tells you otherwise. What actually makes a difference is undertone, and undertone has very little to do with how light or deep your skin is. Two people with completely different complexions can share the same undertone and suit the same colors.

The quick test: look at the inside of your wrist in daylight. Veins that look greenish usually mean a warm undertone; veins that look blue or purple usually mean cool. If you genuinely cannot tell, you are probably neutral, which is the easiest of the three because almost everything works.

Warm undertone

Golden, peach or olive cast. Earth tones are your friends — terracotta, rust, caramel, warm ivory, olive-gold, coral. Cream usually beats stark white.

Cool undertone

Pink, red or bluish cast. Jewel tones sit beautifully — navy, emerald, burgundy, plum, true blue. Pure white and soft grey tend to work better than cream.

Neutral undertone

A balance of both, and the most forgiving. Muted and dusty versions of almost any color will suit you. Choose by setting rather than by undertone.

A second thing matters as much as undertone, and gets talked about less: contrast. Deeper skin tones hold saturated, rich color exceptionally well — emerald, cobalt, mustard, white and bright jewel tones all have real impact, and there is no reason to stick to muted shades. Fairer skin tones can get lost in very pale colors photographed against a bright background, so a deeper anchor color usually helps. In a mixed-tone family, give the richest colors to whoever carries them best rather than putting everyone in the same shade.

The practical version: pick the palette for your setting first, then choose which color in that palette suits each person. Every palette on this page has a warm option, a cool option and a neutral in it, which is deliberate. Nobody in your group needs to be left with a color that does not suit them.

And if you are still unsure, send me a photograph of what you are considering. I photograph families across every complexion in Houston every month, and I will tell you honestly what will work.

Common questions

What if I do not own anything that suits the setting?

Tell me where we are shooting and send photos of what you have. We can usually build an outfit from your own closet, and I will say honestly if one piece is not working.

Can I bring more than one outfit?

Yes. One to three outfits is the sweet spot, and I suggest one relaxed and one dressed up. More outfits means less time in each, so bring only the ones you love.

What should I wear if I am uncomfortable in front of a camera?

Wear what makes you feel like yourself, in colors that suit you. Comfort shows in photographs. I also keep sessions relaxed and unhurried, so you do not have to pose stiffly.

What if it rains or is very hot?

We can reschedule or adjust the time. Houston summers are hot, so choose breathable fabrics and bring water. In cooler months, bring a layer you can add or remove between shots.

Should my family match?

No. Coordinate rather than match. Choose a palette and let each person wear a different color from it.

When should I send you my outfit ideas?

Whenever you like, but a few days before your session works well. Text me a photo and I will tell you what will work.

The night before

  • Steam or iron every outfit, and check for lint, loose threads and stains.
  • Check the forecast and pack a backup layer.
  • Lay out the shoes, jewelry and accessories with the outfit.
  • Bring water, a small mirror, hair ties and pins, and a spare top for little ones.
  • Charge your phone, and text me if the plan changes.
