What to Wear

Colour palettes, shoes and accessories for photographs in Houston — by setting, occasion and season.

Start with the background, not the outfit

Almost every what-to-wear guide starts with colours you like. That is the wrong end. What decides whether an outfit photographs well is what sits behind you — and in Houston that is usually something very green, very bright, or both.

So choose the setting first, take the palette that separates cleanly from it, then let the occasion decide how formal to go. Below is what I tell clients when they ask, which is most of them.

Park or garden

Mercer Arboretum, Terry Hershey, Mandolin Gardens, Oyster Creek — anywhere green.

Cream#F2E8DA
Oatmeal#DCCDBB
Blush#E3BBB0
Terracotta#B5674A
Dusty Navy#3C4A5C

What works

  • Creams, oatmeal and soft neutrals — they lift away from heavy foliage
  • Warm earth tones: terracotta, rust, caramel, clay
  • One deeper anchor colour per group, usually navy or burgundy
  • Light, floaty fabrics — linen, cotton, chiffon

What to avoid

  • Green and olive — you disappear into the background
  • Neon or hot brights, which throw colour onto faces
  • Busy prints and logos, which pull the eye off you
  • Heavy black in daylight — it flattens on camera

Houston note. Houston parks stay green nearly all year, so this holds in January as much as July. Spray for mosquitoes before you dress, not after.

Beach or bay

El Jardin in Seabrook, Galveston, Kings Harbor — anywhere on the water.

Ivory#F6EFE4
Sand#E0CDB2
Sea Glass#AFC4C4
Dusty Rose#D7A99E

What works

  • Ivory, white and sand — they glow in low sun
  • Soft blues and sea-glass greens, which sit naturally against water
  • Long skirts and loose shirts that catch the breeze
  • Flowing fabric — movement is half the picture at the coast

What to avoid

  • Strong black, which goes to silhouette against a bright sky
  • Stiff, structured tailoring — it fights the setting
  • Anything you would be upset to get sand or salt water on
  • Brand-new bright white that has never been washed; it can glare

Houston note. Gulf wind is constant. Bring hair ties, pin anything that lifts, and expect to re-tuck between frames — the wind is usually a gift, not a problem.

Downtown or urban

Buffalo Bayou, Eleanor Tinsley, CityPlace — brick, concrete and glass.

Charcoal#3A3A3C

Burgundy#6E2F39

What works

  • Deeper, richer colour than you would wear in a park
  • Strong tailoring — concrete and glass take structure well
  • Jewel tones: emerald, burgundy, rust, mustard
  • Texture — leather, denim, wool, suede

What to avoid

  • Pale pastels, which wash out against grey concrete
  • Tiny repeating patterns, which shimmer on camera
  • Anything you cannot comfortably walk several blocks in

Houston note. Downtown means walking between backdrops and a lot of heat thrown back off pavement. Bring the shoes you want in the photographs and comfortable ones for the gaps.

Indoors, home or venue

A living room, a hall, a hotel, a restaurant.

What works

  • Colours that work with the room rather than against it — look at the walls first
  • Soft, warm neutrals, which sit well under indoor light
  • A little sheen — silk and satin pick up window light beautifully
  • Layers you can shed once the room warms up

What to avoid

  • Matching everyone in one colour — coordinate, do not uniform
  • Heavy pattern in an already patterned room
  • Fluorescent brights, which shift colour badly under mixed indoor lighting

Houston note. Houston homes run cool with the air conditioning but fill up fast with people. Dress for a room that will be warmer in an hour than when you arrive.

Church or ceremony

A baptism, a christening, a Nikkah, a temple, a courthouse.

Deep Navy#2B3A52

What works

  • Whatever your family’s tradition calls for — that comes first
  • Modest cuts: covered shoulders and knees are expected at most venues
  • Classic, quiet colour rather than anything attention-seeking
  • Clothes you can sit, kneel and stand in repeatedly without adjusting

What to avoid

  • Anything that competes with the people the day belongs to
  • Noisy jewellery — it carries in a quiet room
  • Shoes that echo on a hard floor

Houston note. Check the venue’s own dress guidance before buying anything. If the ceremony is one I have not photographed before, tell me how it runs and I will plan around it.

Wedding

Guests: nothing white, and nothing that reads as white on camera. Wedding party: agree the exact shade together rather than describing it — “navy” covers a lot of ground.

Women

  • A dress with some weight to the fabric so it hangs well through a long day. Check you can dance in it.
  • Shoes: Heels for the ceremony, flats hidden in a bag for the reception. Grass and heels do not mix.

Men

  • A suit that fits properly beats an expensive one that does not. A tie you can loosen later.
  • Shoes: Polished leather, broken in. Dark socks, long enough that nothing shows when you sit.

Accessories

  • One statement piece is plenty. Watches, cufflinks and simple earrings photograph well; anything that catches light repeatedly does not.

Engagement or proposal

Two outfits is the sweet spot: one relaxed, one dressed up. Three is the most I would attempt in a single session.

Women

  • One flowing dress and one everyday outfit you actually feel like yourself in. Movement photographs beautifully.
  • Shoes: If he is proposing, you need to be able to walk there without knowing why. Comfortable shoes.

Men

  • Match her formality, not her colour. If she is in a long dress, you are not in shorts.
  • Shoes: Clean and simple, not brand new. Boots or loafers over trainers.

Accessories

  • For a surprise proposal, keep hands free and nails done — the ring photographs are close and unforgiving.

Maternity

Clothes that show the shape rather than hide it. Fitted beats flowing at the bump, even if the rest flows.

Women

  • A fitted bodice with a long skirt is the classic for a reason. Bring a second option in case you change your mind on the day.
  • Shoes: Flat, every time. You will be standing on grass or sand, and discomfort shows on your face.

Men

  • Simple and quiet — you are the supporting frame. Plain shirt, sleeves rolled.
  • Shoes: Whatever lets you crouch, carry and walk on soft ground.

Accessories

  • Delicate jewellery only. Nothing that sits across the bump or needs constant adjusting.

Family or portraits

Coordinate, do not match. Pick three or four colours from the palette and let everyone take one — it reads as considered rather than staged.

Women

  • Start with the hardest person to dress, usually a teenager, then build everyone else around them.
  • Shoes: Flat or low. You will be sitting on the ground more than you expect.

Men

  • Layers add depth: a shirt with a jacket or cardigan reads better than one flat colour.
  • Shoes: Clean shoes. They end up in more frames than anyone anticipates.

Accessories

  • Dress small children last and bring a spare top for them. Hair ties on wrists show in every photograph — keep them in a pocket.

Headshots

Everything happens from the chest up, so necklines and shoulders are the whole decision.

Women

  • Solid colours, a neckline you are comfortable in, and sleeves — bare shoulders read as a holiday photograph rather than a professional one.
  • Shoes: Irrelevant. Wear whatever is comfortable.

Men

  • A shirt that fits at the collar and the shoulder. A jacket if your industry expects one.
  • Shoes: Also irrelevant. Nobody will ever see them.

Accessories

  • Minimal. Small earrings, a simple watch, nothing that glints. Bring two or three tops so we can compare.

Birthday or celebration

The one occasion to let the guest of honour stand out properly and keep everyone else a notch quieter.

Women

  • Wear the thing you have been saving. If it sparkles, it will photograph well under party lighting.
  • Shoes: If there is dancing, bring a second pair. There is always dancing.

Men

  • Sharper than you think the occasion calls for. You will be glad of it in the pictures.
  • Shoes: Comfortable — parties run longer than planned.

Accessories

  • Sequins, metallics and sparkle work here in a way they do not elsewhere. Lean into it.

Cultural or religious

Traditional dress always photographs better than a Western compromise. Wear what the occasion actually calls for.

Women

  • Full traditional dress where it applies. Rich colour is the point, not something to tone down.
  • Shoes: Easy on and off if shoes come off at the venue, and comfortable for long standing.

Men

  • Match the formality of the ceremony. If the elders are in traditional dress, so are you.
  • Shoes: The same — and check whether you are expected to remove them.

Accessories

  • Family jewellery deserves to be photographed properly. Tell me about anything with a story before we start and I will make time for it.

Corporate or event

Agree a dress code across the team beforehand. Mixed levels of formality are the most common thing that undoes a company photograph.

Women

  • Business or business-casual, matched to your industry. Solid colours over pattern.
  • Shoes: Smart and comfortable — conference days are long and mostly standing.

Men

  • Collared shirt at minimum. A jacket if leadership is wearing one.
  • Shoes: Polished. They show in full-length and group shots.

Accessories

  • Name badges straight and the right way round. Lanyards twist constantly — worth a glance before each group photograph.

Summer (May to September)

Houston summer is the real constraint. Natural fibres only — linen, cotton, light silk — and avoid polyester, which traps heat and shows sweat. Shoot at sunrise or in the last ninety minutes before sunset; midday is unflattering and genuinely uncomfortable. Bring water and a cloth for faces.

Spring or autumn

The best months here. Almost anything works, layers behave, and the light lasts. Spring brings pollen and wildflowers; autumn brings the warmest light of the year. Book these months early — everyone else wants them too.

Winter (December to February)

Mild rather than cold, with lovely low light all day. Layer rather than bundle — coats come on and off between frames. One genuinely cold snap a year is possible, so check the forecast the week before and keep a fallback date in mind.

Does skin tone change what you should wear?

Yes — but not in the way people usually mean. There is no colour that any skin tone cannot wear, and I would be suspicious of any guide that tells you otherwise. What actually makes a difference is undertone, and undertone has very little to do with how light or deep your skin is. Two people with completely different complexions can share the same undertone and suit the same colours.

The quick test: look at the inside of your wrist in daylight. Veins that look greenish usually mean a warm undertone; veins that look blue or purple usually mean cool. If you genuinely cannot tell, you are probably neutral, which is the easiest of the three because almost everything works.

Warm undertone

Golden, peach or olive cast. Earth tones are your friends — terracotta, rust, caramel, warm ivory, olive-gold, coral. Cream usually beats stark white.

Cool undertone

Pink, red or bluish cast. Jewel tones sit beautifully — navy, emerald, burgundy, plum, true blue. Pure white and soft grey tend to work better than cream.

Neutral undertone

A balance of both, and the most forgiving. Muted and dusty versions of almost any colour will suit you. Choose by setting rather than by undertone.

A second thing matters as much as undertone, and gets talked about less: contrast. Deeper skin tones hold saturated, rich colour exceptionally well — emerald, cobalt, mustard, white and bright jewel tones all have real impact, and there is no reason to stick to muted shades. Fairer skin tones can get lost in very pale colours photographed against a bright background, so a deeper anchor colour usually helps. In a mixed-tone family, give the richest colours to whoever carries them best rather than putting everyone in the same shade.

The practical version: pick the palette for your setting first, then choose which colour in that palette suits each person. Every palette on this page has a warm option, a cool option and a neutral in it, which is deliberate. Nobody in your group needs to be left with a colour that does not suit them.

And if you are still unsure, send me a photograph of what you are considering. I photograph families across every complexion in Houston every month, and I will tell you honestly what will work.
